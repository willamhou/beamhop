On destinations and interrupted thoughts

Send the information.Stay where you are.

“They should see this sentence.”
That is the moment Beamhop should be ready for.

Product exploration · Interactive essaySimulated interactions; no connection to your apps

You read a passage on a web page and think of the work underway in Codex. All you need to do is send it there.

First, though, you leave the page, find Codex, look through sessions, check the project, paste, and explain. When you return to the article, you have to find your place again.

There are two searches here: finding the destination before sending, and finding your train of thought afterward. Beamhop should prove its value by making both searches less frequent.

Beamhop gets material into work that is already underway. Reasoning, execution, and output stay with the external agent.

01

You remember the work, but not the session title.

“Codex” is just the app. The real destination is yesterday’s beamhop session about keeping focus in the reading app after sending. With enough sessions, a few similar titles can leave you unsure.

When you recognize a place, you might remember the tree by the door. Work needs recognizable details too: the project, the last thing discussed, or a name you gave it. The product should put those details in view.

Start with session titles alone. Try to find the discussion about keeping focus after sending, then switch to “Show context.” Once you recognize it, make it the current destination.

Context helps you recognize the session. The specific destination you confirm determines where the material goes. Matching titles do not mean matching addresses.

Where do these destinations come from?

This does not assume Beamhop can read every chat in every app. One possible first connection is to register the current session through the receiving tool’s integration while you are already working there, then give it a recognizable name. You can later send from elsewhere without finding it again. Project details and recent messages must also come from information the receiving tool allows it to provide.

The receiving tool needs an addressable session or an intake interface. Unconnected sessions will not simply appear in the list. If stable addressing is unavailable, offer an explicit manual handoff; do not describe opening Codex as delivering to the correct session.

02

Find it once. Spend less time looking next time.

An address book saves us from asking for a number every time. Beamhop can remember a smaller, more specific relationship: send this round of material to the work just confirmed.

After you select a passage, the sending button names the destination. Change it when necessary. Once familiar, a gesture or shortcut could mean “send the selection to the current destination,” without starting with a prompt each time.

The selection says “this.”
The confirmed destination says “there.”

Adding context is itself a meaningful action. Add a note if you want the recipient to act immediately. Supplying material and requesting execution should remain distinguishable.

The example below stays in the app you are reading. Send one passage, switch to the PDF, and send another. See whether you have to find the session again. You can also leave a clip in the corner and decide after reading.

03

Not sure where it belongs? Keep it nearby.

Sometimes all you know is “this might be useful.” Forcing a session choice at that point is another interruption. The corner can work like a few sheets held in your hand: keep the original and its source, then send them together once you know where they belong.

Send directly when you know the destination; save temporarily when you do not. The shelf leaves room for a moment of uncertainty. It should not become a required stop for every send, or take on the job of organizing all your knowledge.

Work can be nonlinear. The same passage can go to a session handling interaction design and another handling implementation. Let each continue its work; Beamhop only needs to remember where the material went. Each connection need not create another topic or fork.

04

The value is in the interruption that never happens.

For Beamhop, the experience most worth refining is small: someone finds evidence in a long article, sends it to the agent already working on it, and reads the next sentence.

Here is a way to check: When someone sends to the same destination a second time, do they spend less time looking? After sending, do they still know what they were reading? Also check how often destinations need correcting and whether a “delivered” status can be verified.

AI can use authorized work context to find candidates and add recognizable summaries to similar sessions. The receiving address must still be established, and a new guess must not silently replace a confirmed destination. Trust depends on that.

return to “why is this passage useful?”

What this demo does and does not show

All sessions, materials, connection states, and receipts are fictional examples. Nothing is sent to Codex or other tools. “Receipt confirmed” means the handoff completed in this demo; it does not mean an agent understood, executed, or finished the work.

The Beamhop repository already includes capture, source previews, external delivery, and recovery paths. Some current delivery methods activate the target app, or only fill its input field and still require submission. The proposals here—addressing a specific session, leaving the foreground unchanged, and confirming receipt—require receiving-tool support. MCP alone does not make them available. Direct-send shortcuts should also establish reliable delivery before reducing the steps.
