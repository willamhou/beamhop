When work branches, stay on the page

Work can branch.Keep your train of thought.

Keep reading.
The clips you have not sent can wait in the corner.

Beamhop essay · 02 / All interactions are simulated

As you read, something reminds you of another task. A new workspace may be the last thing you need.

You want to keep one sentence for an article and pass another to the agent investigating a problem. To hold onto those two thoughts, the tool makes you switch projects, open a materials panel, and organize everything first. The information is still there. You have stopped reading.

This time, the article itself is where you try the interaction. Beamhop appears when you select a passage; saved clips stay in the bottom-right corner. Work can branch without multiplying the interface.

Try it as you read. Select an underlined sample passage below to open its popover. Choose “Keep for later,” then look in the bottom-right corner. Two example sessions are already connected. No real data is sent.
01 / You know where it belongs

Send it. Read the next sentence.

You are reading a test report. This observation would help the session investigating focus behavior.

Sample passage · Reading test notes.pdf, page 4
Select this passage and try sending it directly.

The next sentence is still in the same place. You have not entered another app or put the passage on the shelf first. A small confirmation is enough after the handoff.

The destination names the work you recognize: “Beamhop · Keep reading.” The line below identifies the session that will actually receive it. Find it once, and the next passage takes less searching. Click the destination when you need to change it. Expand the note field when you have something to add.

The name can outlast a session. When the receiving session ends, its replacement address needs to be checked; a similar title is no reason to redirect a clip automatically. Those connection controls do not need to be visible all the time.

02 / You want to keep reading

Saving a clip need not start an organizing session.

Further down, you find another passage. It may be useful, but you do not want to send it yet. You need somewhere to keep it for now.

Sample passage · Codex, delivery discussion
Select this passage, then choose “Keep for later.”

The corner now says “Shelf · 1 clip.” No list opens, no project is created, and no input field takes focus. That one action was enough.

The original text and its source appear when you choose to expand the shelf. Select a few clips, add a note, and send them together. Leave the rest for later. The shelf should be only as large as the task at hand requires.

03 / Another task comes to mind

Hold onto the thought without switching your whole task.

You keep reading and think of an unfinished article. This sentence would work better at its opening.

Sample passage · Safari, writing notes
Send it to “Attention article,” or keep it for later.

If you know where it belongs, change the destination beside the selection and send it. The clips already on the shelf stay there. If you need more time, save this sentence too. A clip intended for a different destination is not automatically selected with the existing batch; choose the clips you want to hand over when you are ready.

Thinking of another task should not require switching to a different “work mode.” The tool needs to remember unfinished selections and notes. It need not ask you to create and name a topic for every thought.

Expand the shelf, deselect a clip, add a note, then collapse it. Read the rest of this section and open it again: your selection and note are still there. Collapsing it just gets the interface out of the way.

04 / See relationships when you need them

Let the tool remember the relationships.

The same test observation can help a product discussion assess a cause and ask an implementer to inspect the code. Those are two handoffs, each with its own destination and note. The original text and source stay the same.

The evidence trail asks what supports a judgment. The work trail asks who is taking it on and what they plan to do. These relationships are worth retaining. Open the handoff history when you want to review them; sending a passage need not begin with a relationship diagram.

AI should follow the same order: narrow the candidates while you are looking for a destination, then respect your confirmed choice. A passage being relevant to two projects does not mean it should be sent twice automatically.

Try sending a passage. This records where material went; it does not generate answers.

Sometimes, being useful means appearing less.

The previous version expanded a temporary shelf into a workspace. It could show more relationships, but it also demanded a shift in attention. That conflicted with the problem Beamhop is meant to solve.

This version leaves each choice to the moment it is needed: selecting text opens the sending popover; saving a clip makes the corner shelf appear; expanding it shows the material. At other times, you are still looking at the article you were reading.

The other task can wait on the shelf.
Keep going with this one.

This essay explores interaction principles, not shipped features. Material, destinations, and receipt confirmations are simulated. The page does not read your system clipboard or connect to real agents; refreshing resets it. Background delivery, stable session addresses, and receipt confirmation still require support from the receiving tool.

Selecting a sample passage stands in for selecting text in a source app. The popover closes when you scroll; the shelf keeps only clips you deliberately save. A receipt confirms a handoff, not that an agent has executed or completed the work.
